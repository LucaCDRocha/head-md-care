# Object Breaking-Reparing Story

## Inspiration:
Kintsukuroi = old Japanese art of repairing broken ceramics using gold, silver, or platinum. It translates to "golden repair", celebrating breakage rather than hiding it.

## Story Version 01
On the final day before her café closes, one of Monika’s  most treasured objects slips from a shelf and shatters on the floor. Not wanting the sadness of the moment to disturb the warmth of the café, she quietly hides the broken pieces behind the counter Tom, a customer visiting for the very first time, gently proposes to take the object home and repair it himself. As Sonia tells him its story, he realizes the object is part of a collection that had silently shaped the soul of the place for years : they were brought here by faithful customers, because here, in this cafe, they feel at home The next day, Tom returns with the restored object in his hands - as if trying to save a small piece of the café before it disappears forever.

<br>

## Story Version 02
Monika's café walls hold twenty years of gifts, each one placed there by a customer who called this their second living room. Tom, visiting for the first time, watches her move between tables with such warmth and passion, that he understands immediately: this isn't a business, it's a family. Then Monika's hand slips and Mrs. Barbara's porcelain teddy bear shatters against the floor, she sweeps the pieces behind the counter with shaking hands, but Tom has already seen the grief, and though he's a stranger here, he feels the urge to offer help for an object that reminds to Monika the presence of the most loyal customer that is not there anymore.

<br>

# Story Version 03 - FINAL
Over the years, Monika's café has become a whole world filled with gifts, but the sad time has now arrived for the shop to close. When the very first object ever given to the café shatters, a new customer named Tom starts putting the pieces back together. As the pieces come back together, the café's beautiful history unfolds, but it also brings us progressively closer to the café’s end.
