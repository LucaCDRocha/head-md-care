**Nina (Customer) | Object: Barny the Frog**

I left Barny the Frog by accident.<br>
I was maybe ... six or seven, and I really didn’t want to go to school. Every morning, Mum would bring me here first for a hot chocolate. That was kind of our deal.<br>
I took Barny everywhere. One day, I must have just … forgotten it. Maria put it on the shelf with a tiny paper cup next to it. She said something like, “He stayed for lunch.”

<br>
<br>

**Mike (Customer) | Object: Coca-Cola Bottle**

I’m not really a café person … never was.<br>
My colleagues and I started coming. We had a construction site down the street. Someone said this place didn’t mind work boots.<br>
I just ordered a Coke. Maria put the bottle down and said, “You’ll want a glass.<br>
We kept coming back. Lunch breaks, mostly. She stopped asking what we wanted. One afternoon, I left the empty bottle. Maria put it on the shelf and said. “So I remember your first order.” … It’s still the same.

<br>
<br>

**Louise (Customer) | Object: Croissant Plate**

This plate … that’s ours. Mine and Lotte’s. We come here every morning, like … eight o’clock. Same table. Two coffees, two croissants.<br>
We always end up ordering the second coffee before we’ve finished the first. Maria still laughs at us.<br>
One morning, Lotte didn’t show up. Maria didn’t ask. She just put another croissant on the plate and said, “In case she’s late.” … Next day, Maria brought the same plate. She's used it for us ever since.

<br>
<br>

**Paul (Customer) | Object: Green Figure**

Every year on Saint Patrick’s Day, Maria does this thing … she turns all the drinks green: Coffee, hot chocolate, lemonade. One year, she even did green milk. The first time I saw it, it looked like a science experiment. We argued about which shade of green was best, as if that actually mattered.<br>
I still don’t know if makes it taste better. But Maria does it anyway … somehow, it became a tradition.

<br>
<br>

**Paul (Daniel) | Object: The Crossword Pencil**

That’s mine or … was, I guess, haha. It started three or four months ago. I took the wrong bus and got off in the rain. Came in the cafe to escape. There was a newspaper on the table. Half of a crossword already done. I told myself I’d just do one clue.<br>
Maria walked past, and said, “You missed one.” After that, I started coming back. <br>
When she sees me, she leaves a newspaper out. The pencil got shorter over time.And then one day I came in, and it was on the shelf. I didn’t need to ask why.
